Android Security Paths

How to collect data from an Android phone, which paths hold secrets and evidence, and how to check for stalkerware and spyware. Without root you work through adb, bug reports and AndroidQF; the /data paths below need root or a full file system extraction.

Overview

Key facts about the current release and how its protections shape what you can collect.

Current releaseAndroid 17, stable since June 2026. Pixels received the QPR2 feature update with the November 2026 security patch.
Support statusDepends on the vendor. Google publishes a monthly security bulletin; Pixel 8 and later and recent Samsung flagships get seven years of updates. Check ro.build.version.security_patch.
File systemRead-only, dm-verity protected system partitions (ext4 or EROFS) and a /data partition (ext4 or F2FS) with file-based encryption split into credential encrypted (CE) and device encrypted (DE) storage.
Built-in security featuresVerified Boot, SELinux, app sandbox per UID, Keystore with TEE or StrongBox, Google Play Protect, Advanced Protection, Private Space, theft protection, Identity Check, developer verification.
Where logs liveIn memory logcat buffers (logd), bug reports, /data/system/dropbox/, /data/tombstones/ and, with Advanced Protection, optional Intrusion Logging.
Access realityThe adb shell user can read /sdcard, dumpsys and settings, not /data/data or /data/system. Unlocking the bootloader to root wipes the device.

Authorized use only: collect from devices you own or have documented consent or legal authority to examine.

Hardening Checklist

Settings that block most real world attacks on Android phones.

  • Buy devices with a long, published update policy and install the monthly patch and Google Play system updates.
  • Keep the bootloader locked and never root a production device.
  • Use a six digit or longer PIN or a password; patterns are easy to shoulder surf.
  • Keep Google Play Protect on and do not sideload apps from links in messages.
  • Turn on Advanced Protection on Android 16 and later for at-risk users, and opt in to Intrusion Logging on Android 17.
  • Turn on theft protection and Identity Check.
  • Review Accessibility, Notification access, Device admin apps and Special app access regularly.
  • Turn off USB debugging and wireless debugging when not in use, and revoke USB debugging authorizations.
  • Set Private DNS to a resolver you trust.
  • Remove user installed CA certificates you do not recognize.
  • Use a work profile or Android Enterprise management for corporate data.
  • Restart the phone regularly; it clears non persistent exploits and returns data to the encrypted BFU state.

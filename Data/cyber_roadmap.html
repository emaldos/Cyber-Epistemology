How to Use the Roadmap

Start from the job you want, not from a list of exams.

  1. Pick a role: filter by category or seniority, or search for a skill or a certification such as OSCP or SC-200.
  2. Read the role: prerequisites come first, then the core certifications employers ask for most, then recommended and optional ones.
  3. Open a certification: select any certification to see its provider, level, exam format, official page and every role that uses it.
  4. Walk the path: use Next roles or the career paths to move from junior to senior, for example SOC Analyst Tier 1 to Tier 2 to Threat Hunter.
  5. Share the view: filters, the selected role and the open certification are kept in the page address, so a bookmark or a shared link opens the same view.

Tip: certifications get you past the CV filter, labs and write-ups get you through the interview. Pair every exam with something you built, defended or broke yourself.

Level Legend

How certifications, roles and priorities are labeled on this page.

Certification levels

FoundationNo experience needed. Proves you know the vocabulary and the basics, for example Security+, ISC2 CC or eJPT.
AssociateJob ready for a first or second security role, usually 0 to 2 years of experience, for example CySA+, BTL1 or SC-200.
ProfessionalEstablished practitioner. Hard practical exams or several years of required experience, for example OSCP+, GCIH or CISA.
ExpertSenior specialist or leadership level, for example OSEP, GREM, CISSP or CISM.

Role seniority

EntryFirst security job, often after help desk, system administration or a degree.
MidTwo to five years in security, works independently.
SeniorLeads investigations or engagements and mentors others.
ExpertTop of a track: research, architecture or leadership.

Priority within a role

PrerequisiteKnowledge to have before you start the role. Skip it if you already have the skills.
CoreThe certifications job ads ask for most for this role. Start here.
RecommendedStrong additions that deepen the role or match a common employer tool set.
OptionalSpecialist, vendor specific or nice to have. Take them when your job uses that area.

Choosing Certifications

Cost, renewal, exam style and what employers actually filter on.

FactorWhat to check
Hands-on versus multiple choicePractical exams (OSCP+, CPTS, BTL1, CKS, BSCP) prove you can do the work. Multiple choice exams (Security+, CISSP, CISM, cloud specialty exams) prove breadth and pass HR filters. Most careers need some of both.
CostCompare the exam price, retake fee, required training and lab time. GIAC with SANS training costs the most. Many employers pay for exams, so ask before you buy.
Renewal and yearly feesSome certifications expire and need continuing education credits and a yearly fee. Budget for the renewal, not only the first exam. See the renewal table below.
Experience requirementsCISSP, CISM, CISA, CCSP and CRISC require years of verified experience. You can pass early and become an associate or wait for the full credential.
Job ads in your regionSearch 20 job ads for the role you want and count which certifications appear. That list beats any ranking.
Government and defense rolesUS DoD roles map certifications to work roles under DoD Manual 8140.03. Check the current qualification matrix before you choose.
Vendor neutral versus vendor specificVendor neutral certifications travel between employers. Vendor certifications (Microsoft, AWS, Splunk, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto) are worth more when the job uses that product.

  • Book the exam date when you start studying. A deadline keeps the plan honest.
  • Keep notes and lab write-ups while you study. They become your portfolio and your interview stories.
